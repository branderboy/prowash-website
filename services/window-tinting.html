WHAT IS WINDOW TINTING?

What is window tinting?

Window tinting is the process of applying a thin film to the inside of your car's windows. This film reduces the amount of sunlight, UV rays, and heat that enters your vehicle. It also gives your car a sleek, customized look. Professional tinting uses high-quality film that won't bubble, peel, or turn purple over time, unlike cheap DIY kits.

Why should I tint my windows?

There are real practical reasons beyond looks. Tinting blocks up to 99% of harmful UV rays that cause skin damage and fade your interior. It reduces heat inside your car by up to 60%, so your AC works less and you stay comfortable. It adds privacy and security: people can't easily see your belongings inside. And in an accident, the film helps hold shattered glass together, adding a layer of safety.

Is window tinting legal in Maryland and DC?

Yes, with limits. In Maryland, front side windows must allow more than 35% of light in. Rear side and back windows can be any darkness. DC has similar rules. Our certified installers know the local laws and will help you choose a tint level that's legal, looks great, and gives you the protection you want. We don't want you getting pulled over.

How long does window tinting take?

Most tinting jobs are done in under 2 hours. A 2-window tint takes about 45 minutes, and a full vehicle (all windows) takes about 1.5-2 hours. Every tint job also includes a free car wash, so your car comes out looking fresh from every angle.

How long does window tint last?

With professional installation and quality film, window tint lasts for years, often the life of the vehicle. That's why we include a lifetime warranty on every installation. If it ever bubbles, peels, or discolors, we'll fix it at no charge. Cheap tint from an inexperienced installer? That might last a year before turning purple. You get what you pay for.

Can I roll down my windows right after tinting?

We recommend waiting 3-5 days before rolling down your newly tinted windows. The film needs time to fully cure and bond to the glass. You might also notice small water bubbles during the first few days, that's completely normal and they'll disappear as the tint cures. After that, your tint is set for life.
